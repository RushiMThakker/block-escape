Privacy Policy for Block Escape

Last updated: November 16, 2026 (applies to version 0.7 and later)

Block Escape ("the app") is a single-player sliding-block puzzle game developed by Rushi Thakker. This policy explains what data the app stores, what Google's advertising and consent libraries in the app do, and the choices you have.

In short. Block Escape has no accounts and no analytics of its own, and the game itself sends nothing to the developer. Your progress stays on your phone. Network traffic comes only from Google's advertising and consent libraries, which show a banner ad below the board, collect some device data (such as the advertising ID and approximate location) for advertising, analytics and fraud prevention, and, where the law requires it, ask for your consent first; and from Google Play's own rating dialog.

Data the app stores on your device

Block Escape does not require an account, login, or any personal information to play. It keeps a small amount of information in its private storage on your phone: which levels you have cleared, which Advent calendar doors you have solved, your settings (seasonal look, vibration, music), whether you have seen the how-to-play guide, and whether the app has already asked once for a rating. The developer cannot read this data and it is not sent to the developer. It is deleted when you clear the app's storage or uninstall the app. If you have Android's own device backup turned on, Android may include it in your backup to your Google account.

If you tap Share, the app opens Android's share sheet with a short text and a link to the game's Play Store page, which you choose whether to send. Rate uses Google Play's in-app review feature: Google Play draws the rating dialog, and the app is not told what you entered.

Advertising and consent (Google)

Block Escape shows a banner ad below the puzzle board. Ads are provided by Google AdMob, using Google's Mobile Ads SDK and its User Messaging Platform (consent) SDK, both bundled in the app.

According to Google's own disclosure for its Mobile Ads SDK, the SDK may collect and share with Google: your device's IP address (which gives an approximate location), interactions with ads such as taps, diagnostic information such as app performance data, and device or other identifiers, including the Android advertising ID and an app-set ID. Google uses this data for advertising, analytics and fraud prevention, and states that it is encrypted in transit. This happens between your device and Google; the developer does not receive it. Google's practices are described in the Google Privacy Policy and in How Google uses information from sites or apps that use its services.

Consent in the EEA, the UK and Switzerland

If you are in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom or Switzerland, Google's consent form asks for your choice before the app requests any ads. Your choice is stored on your device by Google's consent library, so you are not asked again every time. You can change or withdraw it at any time in Settings > Privacy options, which reopens the same form. Without your consent, ads are not personalised, and Google may show limited ads or none. The game works exactly the same either way.

Your choices

  • Consent: where a consent message applies, use Settings > Privacy options to change or withdraw your choice.
  • Advertising ID: you can reset or delete your Android advertising ID, and limit ad personalisation, in your phone's Settings (usually under Google or Privacy, then Ads).
  • Your data in the app: clear the app's storage in Android settings, or uninstall the app, to delete everything it has stored.

Permissions and libraries

  • Internet / network state: needed by Google's advertising and consent libraries (Mobile Ads SDK and User Messaging Platform SDK). The game itself works offline.
  • Advertising ID: used by the Mobile Ads SDK as described above.
  • Vibrate: short haptic feedback for a blocked move and for clearing a level; you can turn it off in Settings. No data is collected through it.

The app does not access your location (beyond the approximate location Google derives from your IP address), contacts, camera, microphone, photos or files.

Children's privacy

Block Escape does not knowingly collect personal information from anyone, including children under 13.

Changes to this policy

If the app's data practices change, for example when a new version adds or removes a library, this page and the date above will be updated.
